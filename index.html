Five Branches of Political Science
CP
Comparative Politics
Examines political systems, institutions, and behaviour across different countries to identify patterns and causes of political outcomes.
IR
International Relations
Studies interactions between states, international organisations, non-state actors, and the global power structures shaping world affairs.
PT
Political Theory
Engages with philosophical questions about justice, rights, liberty, legitimacy, and the proper basis of political authority and obligation.
PP
Public Policy
Analyses how governments design, implement, and evaluate policies to address public problems, and examines their intended and unintended effects.
APB
American / British Politics
In-depth study of specific national political systems, including electoral behaviour, legislative processes, and executive power structures.
PE
Political Economy
Studies the relationship between political processes and economic systems — how power shapes markets and how markets shape political behaviour.
Core Analytical Questions
01
Who governs? — Which individuals, groups, or institutions exercise political power, and under what conditions?
02
Who gets what? — How are political resources, rights, and public goods distributed across society? (Lasswell's definition)
03
Why do states behave as they do? — How do domestic factors, institutions, and international pressures shape state action?
04
What is legitimate authority? — On what basis do governments claim and maintain the right to rule?
Theory & Vocabulary
Key Concepts
Foundational terms and ideas every student of political science must master.
Theories of International Relations
Realism
States are the primary actors in an anarchic world and rationally pursue power and security as their main interests. War is always possible; cooperation is fragile.
IR Theory
Liberalism
International institutions, trade, and democracy reduce conflict. Interdependence raises the cost of war. IGOs create frameworks for durable cooperation.
IR Theory
Constructivism
State interests and identities are socially constructed through interaction. Norms, ideas, and shared beliefs shape behaviour as much as material power.
IR Theory
Marxism / Critical Theory
International politics is driven by class interests and capitalist structures. Dominant states shape the global system to reproduce economic inequalities.
IR Theory
Domestic Politics & Institutions
Sovereignty
The supreme authority of a state within its territorial borders. Both internal (monopoly on force) and external (recognition by other states) dimensions matter.
State Theory
Legitimacy
The normative belief that a ruling authority is rightful and ought to be obeyed. Weber identified three types: traditional, charismatic, and rational-legal.
State Theory
Social Contract
The idea that individuals consent (implicitly or explicitly) to surrender some freedoms to a sovereign authority in exchange for protection and civil order.
Political Theory
Separation of Powers
Division of government authority among distinct branches (executive, legislative, judicial) to prevent tyranny through mutual accountability and checks and balances.
Institutions
Federalism
A division of power between a central government and subnational units (states, provinces), each with constitutionally guaranteed authority in defined areas.
Institutions
Electoral Systems
Rules determining how votes translate into seats. Key types: First Past the Post (FPTP), Proportional Representation (PR), and Alternative Vote / Ranked Choice.

Intellectual History
Key Political Thinkers
The theorists whose ideas shaped how we understand power, justice, and the state.
Classical Foundations
Pl
Plato
428–348 BCE · Ancient Greece
The Republic: justice as harmony; philosopher-kings should rule; typology of regimes from aristocracy to tyranny.
Political Theory
Ar
Aristotle
384–322 BCE · Ancient Greece
Politics: "man is a political animal"; comparative analysis of constitutions; classified 158 regimes; identified polity as the best practicable constitution.
Political Theory Comparative
Ma
Niccolò Machiavelli
1469–1527 · Florence
The Prince: statecraft divorced from ethics; power must be maintained through both force and cunning; realism avant la lettre.
Realism
Ho
Thomas Hobbes
1588–1679 · England
Leviathan: life without sovereign authority is "nasty, brutish and short"; social contract requires absolute sovereign power to maintain peace.
Social Contract
Lo
John Locke
1632–1704 · England
Two Treatises: natural rights to life, liberty, property; government by consent; right to revolution if state violates rights. Foundation of liberal democracy.
Liberalism Social Contract
Ro
Jean-Jacques Rousseau
1712–1778 · Geneva/France
The Social Contract: "general will" as basis of legitimate authority; popular sovereignty; human nature corrupted by society, not inherently sinful.
Social Contract
Modern & Contemporary
We
Max Weber
1864–1920 · Germany
Three types of legitimate authority: traditional, charismatic, rational-legal. State holds the monopoly on the legitimate use of physical force within a territory.
State Theory
Gr
Antonio Gramsci
1891–1937 · Italy
Concept of hegemony: ruling class maintains power through cultural and ideological consent, not only coercion. Civil society as the site of counter-hegemonic struggle.
Marxism
Ra
John Rawls
1921–2002 · USA
A Theory of Justice: "veil of ignorance" thought experiment; difference principle — inequalities permitted only if they benefit the least advantaged.
Liberal Theory
Hu
Samuel Huntington
1927–2008 · USA
Clash of Civilizations: post-Cold War conflict driven by cultural and religious identity rather than ideology. Influential and heavily contested.
IR Theory
Comparative Politics
Systems of Government
Structural typologies used to classify how political authority is organised and exercised.
Executive–Legislative Relationships
Presidential Systems
President is head of state and government, elected independently of the legislature. Strict separation of powers; fixed terms; no vote of no confidence.
Examples
USA Brazil Mexico Indonesia South Korea
Parliamentary Systems
Government drawn from and accountable to the legislature. Prime minister can be removed by vote of no confidence; executive and legislative power fused.
Examples
UK India Canada Germany Japan
Semi-Presidential Systems
Dual executive: a directly-elected president coexists with a prime minister accountable to parliament. Power distribution varies greatly between cases (cohabitation possible).
Examples
France Finland Portugal Taiwan
Authoritarian Systems
Concentrated power without meaningful accountability. Sub-types include single-party states, military juntas, competitive authoritarian regimes, and personalised dictatorships.
Examples
China Russia Saudi Arabia North Korea

Political History
Landmark Events
Key events in modern political history that shaped the international order and political thought.
Modern State Formation
1648
Peace of Westphalia
Treaties ending the Thirty Years' War. Established the principle of state sovereignty and non-interference, creating the foundations of the modern state system.
1776
American Declaration of Independence
Articulated Lockean natural rights in a founding document; first major modern democratic republic. Influential template for constitutions worldwide.
1789
French Revolution
Overthrew the ancien régime; introduced mass political mobilisation, nationalism, and ideological politics (left-right spectrum originated in the National Assembly).
1848
Revolutions across Europe / Communist Manifesto
Wave of liberal and nationalist uprisings. Marx and Engels publish their critique of capitalism and call for working-class revolution.
1917
Russian Revolution
Bolshevik seizure of power creates first communist state. Defined the ideological conflict of the 20th century and catalysed global revolutionary movements.
1945
End of WWII & UN Founding
Post-war order established: UN Charter, Nuremberg principles, beginning of decolonisation wave, and bipolar Cold War competition between USA and USSR.
Contemporary Order
1948
Universal Declaration of Human Rights
UN General Assembly adopts UDHR; first international articulation of individual rights as a global norm, laying the foundation for international human rights law.
1957–1975
African Decolonisation Wave
Rapid decolonisation transforms the international system; over 40 African states gain independence, fundamentally reshaping the UN and global power dynamics.
1989–1991
End of the Cold War
Fall of the Berlin Wall (1989), dissolution of the Soviet Union (1991). Third Wave of democratisation; "unipolar moment" of US hegemony begins.
2001
9/11 and the War on Terror
Attacks reshape security policy, international law, and civil liberties globally. Non-state actors (Al-Qaeda, then ISIS) emerge as central IR actors.
2008–2010
Global Financial Crisis
Economic crisis accelerates shifts in political economy, fuels populist and anti-establishment movements in Europe and the Americas, reshaping party systems.
2016–present
Democratic Backsliding & Populism
V-Dem records more countries autocratising than democratising for the first time since tracking began. Populist governments challenge liberal norms in multiple democracies.

Skills You Develop
Analysis
Evaluating evidence, constructing arguments, critiquing sources, and synthesising information from multiple perspectives.
Writing
Clear, structured, evidence-based writing — essays, policy briefs, briefing notes, and reports under pressure.
Research Methods
Quantitative and qualitative methods, data literacy, and the ability to design a research question and evaluate evidence.
Communication
Presenting and defending arguments verbally, translating complex ideas for non-specialist audiences.
Global Literacy
Understanding of international institutions, geopolitics, comparative systems, and cross-cultural political contexts.
Critical Thinking
Recognising assumptions, identifying logical fallacies, and evaluating competing normative and empirical claims.
